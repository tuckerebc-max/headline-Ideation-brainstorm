# Examples

Version: 0.1.0

These examples are synthetic. They demonstrate the method and are not templates to
copy mechanically.

## Example A: reported essay

Brief: A reported essay for district leaders explains how a small urban school
changed its student discussion practice. The evidence supports a modest improvement
in participation, not a universal result. The author's voice is plain, observant, and
skeptical of grand claims. Medium: web article; length limit is unverified.

Raw field excerpts:

| Option | Angle | Form | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What Changed When Every Student Had to Enter the Conversation | point_result | declarative | shortlist |
| A Discussion Routine That Made More Room for Quiet Students | reader_utility | declarative | shortlist |
| Can a Classroom Routine Change Who Gets Heard? | tension | question | shortlist |
| The Small Rule Behind a Bigger Conversation | image_detail | metaphor | revise: too vague without subtitle |
| The Revolutionary Method That Transformed Student Voice | point_result | declarative | reject: inflated and unsupported |

Why the first three work: each makes a different honest promise; each preserves the
modest scope; each gives district leaders a reason to read. The second is the most
useful, the third the most open-ended, and the first the clearest report frame.

## Example B: practical newsletter

Brief: A weekly newsletter helps nonprofit managers decide which meeting to cancel.
The author's voice is direct, dry, and slightly funny. Medium: email subject line;
the sender has not supplied a character limit.

Candidate set:

- The Meeting That Can Become an Email
- Before You Schedule Another Meeting, Ask This
- A Small Test for Meetings That Have Outlived Their Use
- What Are We Actually Meeting To Decide?
- Cancel the Meeting. Keep the Decision.

The last line is energetic but may sound too absolute if the source only discusses
one category of meeting. Its release status is ready_with_conditions pending the
scope decision. The fourth is safest when the newsletter is explanatory.

## Example C: source unavailable

Request: "Give me 25 punchy titles for my report on the new program."

Result: needs_input. Ask for the report or a source summary, audience, purpose, genre,
medium, and must-preserve claims. The skill may offer clearly labeled form prompts,
but it must not release a factual shortlist.
