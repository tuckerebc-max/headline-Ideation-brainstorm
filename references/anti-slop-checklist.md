# Anti-Slop Checklist

Version: 0.1.0

Use this as a contextual screen. A flagged phrase is not automatically forbidden:
the question is whether the pattern is earned by this source, audience, and voice.

| ID | Pattern | Flag when | Repair or disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AS-01 | Generic abstraction | Could title thousands of unrelated works | Replace abstraction with a source detail, claim, or reader payoff |
| AS-02 | Formulaic promise | Uses a reusable template with no specific payload | Keep the form only if the payload is concrete and true |
| AS-03 | Inflated certainty | Uses always, never, proves, revolutionizes, game-changing, or similar without support | Restore the source's qualifier or reject |
| AS-04 | False urgency | Implies now-or-never or crisis without evidence | State the actual consequence or remove urgency |
| AS-05 | Deceptive gap | Withholds the central fact to manufacture curiosity | Name enough context for an honest promise |
| AS-06 | Clickbait overclaim | Teases a revelation the work does not deliver | Align the headline with the delivered payoff |
| AS-07 | Keyword stuffing | Repeats search terms or appends terms unnaturally | Use one natural term when useful; route SEO tradeoffs |
| AS-08 | Canned AI vocabulary | Uses interchangeable phrases such as "the future of", "unlocking", "a new era", or "what you need to know" without source-specific reason | Replace with concrete language or reject |
| AS-09 | Corporate uplift | Sounds like a launch announcement or motivational deck rather than the work | Restore the author's stance and actual subject |
| AS-10 | Vague demonstrative | Relies on "this", "these", "what happened next", or "the truth about" without a referent | Add the referent or reject |
| AS-11 | Empty superlative | "Best", "ultimate", "most important", or similar without a defensible basis | Use a precise claim or remove |
| AS-12 | Templated rhythm | Repeated three-part cadence, colon formula, or paired opposites across many options | Retain only when voice or content earns it |
| AS-13 | Voice substitution | Replaces the author's diction, humor, cultural register, or point of view | Restore voice or mark for author decision |
| AS-14 | Search-first distortion | Serves a keyword at the cost of accurate human meaning | Prefer people-first clarity; record SEO decision |
| AS-15 | Mysteriousness without payoff | Sounds intriguing but gives no reason to read and no recoverable subject | Add subject, detail, or reader benefit |

## Self-check questions

- Would a reader know what kind of work this is?
- Does the headline represent the actual work rather than a generic content type?
- Is the most energetic word earned by evidence or merely decorative?
- Does the line sound like this author when read beside source excerpts?
- Could the line be transplanted to five unrelated works without changing it?
- Does the title create curiosity by exposing a worthwhile question, or by hiding
  information the reader needs?
- If search terms were removed, would the line still make sense to a person?
- Is a short line genuinely clear, or merely incomplete?

## Repair order

1. Fix factual or promise problems.
2. Restore missing subject and reader value.
3. Remove generic or inflated language.
4. Restore voice and useful rhythm.
5. Address length and medium constraints.
6. Polish punctuation only after meaning and fit are stable.
